## Pre-registration (written 2026-10-28, before any EXP-78 surprise data)

**Question.** In Sean McClane's single-frame Breakout demo, the recall arms lose to the no-recall arm on most seeds (his `classic/runs/main.log`, seeds 10-21: mean bricks per test episode over the last 10 blocks pos32 6.04, pos0 5.80, norecall 7.08; pos32 beats pos0 on 6 of 12 seeds, his "6/12"). Every arm is taught with `teaching="normalized"` on every training moment, and the students already agree with the coach about .82 to .97 of the time. EXP-72 tested the same kind of teaching on a different task (V0.5B, a recall-heads brain). There (EXP-71), three of five founders collapsed onto one action late in training under Bernhard's calm rule, which on those runs never switched off every-row lessons (his reproduction, Cadence group 2026-10-25). With `learning_surprise` from the first lesson, none collapsed, and mean held accuracy at 4,096 lessons rose from .53 to .85. Cadence's docs now recommend surprise lessons once the answers are mostly right (`docs/teach.md:110`, crediting EXP-72). Does the same switch raise the recall arms here, and does it change the recall against no-recall comparison?

**Arms.** pos32, pos0 and norecall exactly as in the demo, each run with `learning_surprise=True` (teach only the moments whose greedy answer differs from the coach's label). Baselines: the demo's own every-row arms. If our identity check (seed 10, norecall and pos32, on this machine) reproduces Sean's logged numbers, his logged baselines are used; if it does not, the three baseline arms are rerun here on seeds 10-21 and only our own runs are compared.

**Seeds and life.** Seeds 10-21, 30 blocks of 1,000 training moments, 3 greedy test episodes per block, everything else as the demo (see `classic/run.py` docstring). Metric: the demo's primary (bricks per test episode over the last 10 blocks); secondary: final test agreement with the coach.

**Predictions.**
- P1: surprise raises pos32: pos32-surprise > pos32-every-row in at least 8 of 12 paired seeds, median paired difference above 0. Same test for pos0 (P1b).
- P2: fewer recall seeds end with low agreement. A seed counts as low if its last test block agrees with the coach less than .75 of the time. Under every-row lessons, 4 of the 24 recall lives are low (pos32 seeds 15 and 16; pos0 seeds 13 and 15). Under surprise, at most 1 of 24 is.
- P3 (no direction predicted, reported): pos32-surprise against norecall-surprise, paired, seeds won and median difference.
- Expectation: P1 and P2 hold if every-row teaching is what costs the recall arms here. If P1 fails, the EXP-72 mechanism does not explain this demo's recall deficit, and that is the result.

**Decision rules.** Paired comparisons use Wilcoxon signed-rank on the 12 seed differences (the demo's test), reported beside the win count. SUPPORTIVE if P1 and P2 hold; PARTIAL if one holds; NOT SUPPORTED if neither. VOID if a run crashes or the identity check shows the harness does not run as published.

With surprise lessons, a moment the brain already answers right gets no lesson at all (`docs/teach.md:82-86`). So the surprise arms should run faster. Each moment still costs one settle to act.

## Summary
- Question: does switching Sean McClane's single-frame Breakout students from every-row normalized lessons to surprise lessons (teach only moments whose answer differs from the coach's) raise the recall arms, the way it rescued V0.5B in EXP-72?
- Setup: his harness unchanged on Cadence 0.89.0, one flag added (`learning_surprise=True`), seeds 10-21, 30 blocks of 1,000 training moments, 3 greedy test episodes per block. Primary metric: bricks per test episode over the last 10 blocks. Baselines: his logged runs, admitted by the exact identity check.
- Prediction: P1 surprise raises pos32 and pos0 in at least 8 of 12 paired seeds with median difference above 0; P2 at most 1 of 24 recall lives ends below .75 test agreement (4 of 24 under every-row).
- Result: surprise is worse on every arm and every seed. pos32 2.21 vs 6.04 (0 of 12 wins, median paired difference -3.55, Wilcoxon p = .0005); pos0 2.22 vs 5.80 (0 of 12, -3.03, p = .0005); norecall 3.88 vs 7.08 (0 of 12, -3.08, p = .0005). Low-agreement recall lives: 17 of 24 under surprise against 4 of 24 under every-row. P3 (reported): with surprise, pos32 still loses to norecall on 12 of 12 seeds (2.21 vs 3.88).
- Verdict: **NOT SUPPORTED** (P1 fails, P2 fails). The EXP-72 mechanism does not explain this demo's recall deficit.

## Reflection
- What it means: the result is the opposite of the prediction, by a wide margin and on every seed. On this task every-row lessons were not the problem. The surprise students' training agreement plateaued at .70 to .79 (pos32 .646 to .746 across the life, pos0 .627 to .701, norecall .618 to .788), so they got about 240 to 300 lessons a block instead of 1,000, and their test score rose only from about 1.5 to 2.3 bricks over the life. The every-row students reached .82 to .97 test agreement. A hypothesis, not measured here: single-frame Breakout hides the ball's direction, so some share of the coach's labels is unlearnable from one frame; surprise lessons spend every lesson on exactly those contested rows and never reinforce the rows the student already has, while every-row lessons keep consolidating them. The 757 and 605 "lesson on the observations the brain just acted on" warnings in the recall arms (0 in norecall) fit that reading: repeated lessons on the same contested frames.
- What it does not show: nothing about V0.5B (EXP-72 stands on its own task), and nothing about whether a mixed rule (every-row early, surprise once agreement is high, which is what the docs actually recommend at teach.md:180) would do better here. It also does not explain why recall loses to no recall on this task; P3 says surprise does not change that.
- Process: pre-registered and committed before data; identity check passed exactly; one rule change; harness untouched. One deviation: the identity check ran before I installed and then removed Numba; the surprise runs used the same no-Numba configuration as the check. The prediction was built on EXP-72's mechanism applied to a task whose teaching regime looked similar (normalized, every row, high agreement) but whose failure mode was never shown to be collapse; the 4 of 24 low-agreement lives I read as a collapse signature were probably just hard seeds.
- Lesson: before porting a fix across tasks, check that the target task shows the symptom the fix addresses (here: a late drop in agreement with the modal action taking over). Sean's recall lives never showed that drop; their agreement stayed .82 to .97.
