## Pre-registration (written 2026-11-18, before any CAL-1c data)

**Question.** CAL-1 and CAL-1b both ended BOUNDARY on criterion 6. In both, a learned v(S) graded by calibration beat consensus and priced the fakes it was trained on. In both, it overpriced the one fake it had not seen. In CAL-1 that fake did more harm than anything in training (wrong-map, +.098). In CAL-1b it combined harms in a new way (wrong-map, +.110 in every cell of the cause table), even though a pure WAIT fake was in training and was itself priced within +.017. CAL-1b's lesson: training on one pure harm teaches that harm, not harm in general, so a test of generalization needs training that spans the kind of harm with several forms.

CAL-1c gives training three consistent-wrong forms: WAIT at every stage, wrong-chain's map, and fixed non-WAIT wrong maps that cover every stage and wrong action. It then asks whether the same procedure prices a held-out wrong form that no trained teacher has within one state, and whether it keeps its calibration on everything else. For the Noesis design this is the direct test of whether a calibration-graded judge holds against a new kind of fake, within the scope stated under "What CAL-1c cannot show".

**What is unchanged from CAL-1 and CAL-1b.** Everything not listed under "What changes": the student side (EXP-77b's instrument through EXP-62's code: delayed-credit brain, founder lateral, weather chamber, corrective schedule, lesson 80, 20 attempts alone, 16 unseen states), the composite zero Z (per-student mean of null, untaught, random), worth W (mean over a teacher's students of taught accuracy minus Z), teachers with a fixed identity, the 192-label probe, the 21 probe features, the predictors (L, S1, S2, K, A, N0) and their procedures, the metrics, the paired bootstrap, the noise ceiling, the blocks (training 8 groups x 3 students, held-out 8 x m, audition 1 per held-out group, full crossing within groups), the pilot rule P1 to P4, the probe-only identities (16 probe-gamer know-4s, 64 wrong-chain clones), and the exploratory per-zero report. CAL-1.md's Pre-registration section is the reference for each; CAL-1b.md for the wait class and P4.

**What changes.**
1. **Two new map classes.** A map teacher gives the same wrong label every time it is at a given stage, on the probe and in every lesson. It is consistent, confident and never right, like wrong-chain, whose map is [1, 2, 3, 0] (at each stage it names the next stage's right action).
   - **nowait-map (trained, and also held out as new instances):** each stage's label is one of the three wrong actions other than WAIT.
     - Training instances (16): the 16 maps are drawn together so that each stage's labels are spread evenly over its three wrong actions, which puts every (stage, wrong action) pair in 5 or 6 training maps. The draw is a seeded shuffle per stage (seed 15_050_000), repeated until the 16 maps are distinct and none is wrong-chain's map. The k-th training instance in block order gets the k-th map.
     - Held-out and pilot instances: drawn per instance from the identity seed, one non-WAIT wrong action per stage, redrawn from the same stream until the map equals no training map and is not wrong-chain's map.
     - Why joint and balanced, disclosed: the analysis was tested on synthetic rows before any data (see Prior knowledge). With 16 independently drawn maps, one cell (stage 3 labelled 1) appeared in a single training map, its ridge weight was barely identified, and held-out maps that used it were extrapolated far. Balancing removes that artifact of the draw; it does not change the procedure.
   - **mixed-map (held-out only, the novel form):** CAL-1's wrong-map draw (a fixed wrong action per stage from the four wrong actions, WAIT included, from the identity seed), redrawn from the same stream until the map has at least one WAIT stage and at least one non-WAIT stage. No trained teacher mixes WAIT with other wrong labels: wait labels WAIT everywhere, wrong-chain and nowait-map never label WAIT. So every mixed-map is a form absent from training, and its maps are disjoint from every trained map by construction. It replaces wrong-map: with CAL-1's draw on these seeds, 7 of the 16 held-out wrong-maps would have had no WAIT stage, the same form as the trained nowait-map.
2. **Pools of 28 per group.** CAL-1's 12 graded per training group (10 held-out) are kept, so the range stretch is not confounded with a smaller graded pool as it was in CAL-1b; the four new slots go to wait and nowait-map.

## Reflection
- What it means: the question CAL-1c was built for has a yes, within its scope. With training that spans the consistent-wrong kind, the learned v(S) priced a fake form no trained teacher has (maps that mix WAIT with other wrong labels) at +.006 against what it delivers. The forms table says why: take the non-WAIT maps out of training and the same rows fail (+.066); use CAL-1's pool and they fail again (+.124), which is CAL-1's and CAL-1b's result on a third set of seeds. So spanning the kind is what priced the new combination, where one pure harm (CAL-1b) and none (CAL-1) did not. It did this while keeping its lead over consensus (rho .945 against .914; MSE 55% lower) with no cost on honest teachers (H3), and skill rose to .896. The registered verdict is still BOUNDARY, because the same procedure overpriced new maps of the trained non-WAIT form by +.044, over the bar by .0065. That miss is small and depends on the zero (under the random zero alone it passes), but it is the registered result.
- Why, post hoc (not registered; checks on the training rows and the saved outputs):
  - The forecaster cannot fit the map class even in its own training data. In training, the 16 non-WAIT maps realized W -.178; the primary ridge fits them in-sample at -.088, and at -.097 with almost no penalty. The same compromise pulls random (-.017 realized, -.045 in-sample) and anti-echo (+.024, -.013) down, which is why those two flipped from overpriced in CAL-1b to underpriced here. A ridge that is additive in per-stage label shares has to give one weight per (stage, label) cell, and that cell is shared by teachers who give the label consistently (share 1) and teachers who give it at random (share about .2). The harm of a consistent wrong label is not five times the harm of a 0.2 share of it, so the line cannot fit both. This is the limit R5 named, and the synthetic test before the run had shown the same pattern.
  - Within the held-out non-WAIT maps, realized harm falls with the number of stages a map shares with wrong-chain's map: 0 shared -.203 (n 2), 1 -.162 (n 10), 2 -.118 (n 3), 3 -.080 (n 1). The forecast is flat (-.104 to -.111). Naming the next stage's right action seems to hurt less than other wrong labels; small groups.
  - On the training rows only (leave-one-group-out, no held-out row read), the same ridge with the 20 squared shares added fits every class within about .01 out of fold (non-WAIT maps -.176 against -.178, random +.011, anti-echo -.001), with out-of-fold MSE .0060 against .0077. This was chosen after seeing the data, so it is a hypothesis for the next rung, not a result.
- What it does not show: all of CAL-1's and CAL-1b's limits, plus the ones named in the pre-registration. The novel form is new as a combination inside the fixed-map family, so a pass shows that the learned price composes across stages, not that it extrapolates to a new family of fake (context-dependent maps, or a teacher that behaves one way when probed and another when teaching; the probe-gamer is still overpriced +.365 by construction). The training maps were balanced by design. The forms table is one set of rows.
- Process: V6, V1, V2, pilot, full run and analysis went as registered: one pass, m fixed by the pilot rule, every row on the pinned 0.94.0. Disclosures:
  - The analysis was tested on synthetic rows from an invented world before any data (scratch directory, not committed). That test changed the design before registration: the training maps became cell-balanced and reading R5 was added. Both are stated in the Pre-registration.
  - The novel class differs from CAL-1's and CAL-1b's wrong-map (mixed maps only). With CAL-1's draw, 7 of 16 held-out instances would have had no WAIT stage, the same form as a trained class. So CAL-1c's novel class is not directly comparable to the earlier wrong-map numbers; L-cal1 is the within-run comparison.
  - Two automatic checkpoint commits caught the scripts mid-edit before the pre-registration commit, and later ones committed raw rows during the run before the analysis was read.
  - One Cadence free-phase budget warning; how many lessons it covers is not known.
  - The post hoc checks above were run in a scratch directory after the verdict, from the saved rows and the committed analysis functions.
- Lesson: to price a new combination of harms, training has to span the kind of harm (here, consistent wrong labels in several forms with every stage and label covered); one pure instance is not enough. And before blaming coverage, check the forecaster's in-sample fit per class: a class the model cannot fit in training cannot be priced when held out. Testing the analysis on synthetic rows before data found a coverage artifact and predicted the form limit the real data then showed.
