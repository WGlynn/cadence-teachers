Cadence Lab Atlas · reflections EXP-01 to EXP-55

Small brains, learning to teach each other

We grow small Cadence brains and ask whether one can measurably teach another, and whether the worth of that teaching can be priced from outcomes instead of from what the brain looks like inside. The goal is a market that pays for teaching ability passed down generations, where credit follows the lineage of what was learned. This atlas collects every reflection so far, the wins and, just as proudly, the nulls, voids and our own retracted mistakes.

EXP-10Per-mind worth attribution in mixed collaborationCROSSEDattribution (M1-M5)Inside a single collaboration, transfer credited genuine minds +0.375 on average and docked fakes -0.479, a +0.854 attribution gap.
Question
In one collaboration with genuine and fake minds of identical interior structure, does transfer localize worth to the specific genuine minds, and does aggregate transfer scale with the genuine fraction?
Dates
2026-10-08 (iter5 run, iter8 fixed rerun) (iter5, iter8)
Verdict note
M2 additive half, on corrected iter8 numbers; iter5 superseded (verdict held and strengthened). Additive per-node attribution only; linearity largely by construction; XOR-style synergy still open.
What it means
within a single collaboration, transfer assigns positive credit to the minds whose blocks carry real T-information and negative credit to the ones that teach the opposite, and aggregate credit is linear in the number of genuine minds. That is the per-contributor property a Shapley-style split needs, shown in the simplest additive case.
Lesson
An additive task can only demonstrate additive attribution; collaborative-worth claims need a task where worth exists only in the combination, and fakes should include a zero-information arm, not just an opposite-teaching one.
