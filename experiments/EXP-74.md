# EXP-74: does switching on learning_surprise mid-training give normalized teaching an oversized step?

- Date: 2026-10-25 | Cadence version: 0.88.0, main `b6fdc83` | Program iter: none (mechanism probe for EXP-71's late collapse; Cadence group thread 2026-10-25)
- Script: `exp/EXP-74_calm_switch_step_spike.py` | Evidence: `exp/EXP-74-<seed>.jsonl` (one row per lesson), `exp/EXP-74.log`

## Summary
- Question: is the late collapse in EXP-71 caused by an oversized weight step when the calm rule switches `learning_surprise` on? In EXP-71, founder 59101 converged under Bernhard's calm rule: taught .99 and held .83 at 2048, with calm on from lesson 1864. By 4096 it had collapsed: taught .39, with 169 of 192 answers on one action. Founder 59204 entered calm at 1682 and also regressed.
- What the code does (`learning.py` at 0.88.0, `_update`): with `teaching="normalized"`, each lesson's contrast is averaged over the taught rows. It is then divided, per synapse, by a running RMS of past contrasts (forgetting factor .99).
- The hypothesis (not yet measured): under every-row teaching, a mostly-right batch averages a few large wrong-row contrasts with many smaller right-row ones, so the running RMS settles small. With surprise on, only wrong rows are taught, so the averaged contrast is undiluted. Divided by the small RMS, it makes a large step until the RMS catches up. The probe measures whether right rows really contribute small contrasts; if they do not, the hypothesis fails here.
- Setup: replay of EXP-71's H2 training for 59101, 59204 and 59203 (the control, converged and held), 4096 lessons, Bernhard's runner and calm rule unchanged. Each lesson logs rows taught, wrong count on its 16 patterns, the running level, the surprise flag, and the learner's `scale_step` (mean absolute weight step) and `bias_step`. Taught and held accuracy on EXP-71's panels at 2048 and 4096 check the replay.
- Prediction (written before running):
  - Replay check: taught and held at 2048 and 4096 equal EXP-71's for each founder.
  - SPIKE (mechanism supported) needs two things, in both 59101 and 59204. First, the step jumps: in the 50 lessons after calm first engages, the mean `scale_step` is at least twice the mean over the 50 lessons before. Only lessons that taught at least one row count, because a surprise lesson with no wrong row runs no update. Second, the brain gets worse soon after: the running level rises back above .02 within 200 lessons of the switch.
  - REJECTED if that ratio is below 1.25 for both.
  - Otherwise INCONCLUSIVE. The control 59203 is reported. If it spikes too without collapsing, the spike is not sufficient, and the write-up says so.
- Result (Windows, numpy 2.4.2, OMP_NUM_THREADS=1; finished 22:33 CT). Replay check: taught and held at 2048 and 4096 equal EXP-71's exactly for all three founders (e.g. 59101 2048 .9896/.8333, 4096 .3906/.3542), so EXP-71's deepcopy readings did not perturb training. Mean `scale_step` over taught lessons, 50 lessons after vs before calm first engaged: 59101 .55x (7 vs 50 taught lessons; calm at 1864), 59204 .43x (calm at 1682, off again at 1683), 59203 4.97x (calm at 1260, off at 1268, on from 1919). Level back above .02 within 200 lessons: 59101 at 2310 (446 lessons, outside the window), 59204 at 1683, 59203 at 1268.
- Exploratory, not predicted (read from the logged fields): zero-step nudged phases. A taught lesson whose nudged phase ran 0 steps has `nudged_steps` 0 with `nudged_residual` about .0025, under the runner's `learning_tolerance` .003. In brain.py `_equilibrate_local` loops only while some row's residual exceeds the tolerance, so such a phase never relaxes toward the label. Share of taught lessons with a zero-step nudged phase, per 256 lessons: none before 1280 in any founder. 59101: 45% at 1536-1792, then 68%, 43%, 75%, and 100% from 3328, while wrong rose to about 8.5 of 16 and the mean step fell to 4e-7, 1e-16 and 3e-28. 59204: 30% at 1792-2048, 67%, 97%, then 100% from 3328, wrong about 8.5, step to 6e-40. 59203: 49% at 1536-1792; it reached 0 wrong near 2048 and calm then taught nothing to 4096. Over lessons from 512 on that taught, zero-step lessons averaged 7.45 / 7.81 / 5.26 wrong rows (59101 / 59204 / 59203), against 4.17 / 4.09 / 3.13 where the nudged phase ran. No lesson was refused.
- Verdict: REJECTED (the oversized-step-at-the-switch hypothesis: ratio below 1.25 in both collapsing founders). The control spiked without collapsing.

## Reflection
- What it means: the calm switch does not jolt the learner; my mechanism was wrong. What the logs show instead is that learning stops. From about lesson 1300 the nudged phase increasingly starts inside the solver's tolerance and takes no steps, and in the two collapsed founders it reached every lesson, with the weight step decaying geometrically while half the rows were wrong. Whether a founder ends right or collapsed then depends on where it was when learning stopped: 59203 was at 0 wrong and calm froze it there. This reading is post hoc. Zero-step phases go with more wrong rows, but which causes which is not shown here. EXP-75 tests whether lowering the tolerance removes the collapse. It also changes how EXP-72 should be read: teaching only wrong rows from lesson 0 reached 1.00 taught by 1024, before zero-step phases appear, and then stopped updating. It may avoid the death by arriving first rather than prevent it.
- Process: pre-registered and pushed before data (ed76b33); replay verified exactly. The per-lesson log was meant for step sizes and happened to carry the solver fields that showed the real pattern; logging the full report, not only the metric of interest, is what made this visible.
- Lesson: when a learner goes quiet, log the solver's own fields (steps, residual, tolerance) per lesson. A phase that "converges" in zero steps is not settling; it is the tolerance ending the phase before it starts.
- Next: EXP-75 (`learning_tolerance` .0003 under the calm rule). If it holds, the group reply carries a measured mechanism and a knob; if not, it says what was ruled out.

## Addendum 2026-10-25 23:45 CT (Bernhard's independent diagnosis)
- Bernhard, reproducing on his machine: neither 59201 nor 59204 reached the .02 switch there, so every lesson taught every row. Normalized steps do not shrink once an answer is right, so lessons keep pushing rows already answered right until the nudge cannot move the readout: its effect on the motor outputs fell about 100x and the step went from about 1e-4 to 1e-37, after which one lapse sticks. Swapping parts between a healthy and a collapsed copy puts the damage in the cortex weights, not the heads' address maps. This places the cause upstream of the zero-step phase: the nudge's shrinking effect is what took the nudged phase's starting residual under the .003 tolerance. The zero-step phase is the symptom that made the collapse permanent here, not its origin.
